    Why this is not delivery

    Delivery moves an existing dish to a new address. The Virtual Buffet moves the buffet experience itself — choosing, composing, sitting down to a full table.

    ModelWhat the guest buysWhat limits it
    Dine-in buffet A grilled main plus the whole 150-item salad bar, from THB 259 Only works at a branch, and only during opening hours
    Food delivery One dish, ordered and eaten alone No composition, no occasion, price competition on every single item
    Virtual Buffet A curated set the guest composes across eight categories Needs packaging and kitchen sequencing to hold quality on arrival

    Who it is for

    Three groups already pay a premium for food that feels both indulgent and clean. The set format serves all three without changing the kitchen.

    Working professionals

    Want a proper meal after a long day but will not travel for it. Buy single and double sets on weekday evenings.

    Health-minded households

    Follow the organic salad and clean-eating category. Choose lighter mains and treat the salad as the centre of the set, not the side.

    Small gatherings and offices

    Need one order that feeds a table and looks presentable on arrival. The largest basket size and the most repeatable occasion.

    Positioning statement

    For people who want restaurant-quality food that still tastes like real food, Grill & Ground is the buffet you compose yourself and receive complete — premium grill, organic ground — available wherever you are, not only where the restaurant stands.

    One menu, one price, every channel.

    Nothing is re-priced for the internet. A main course costs what the menu book says it costs. The buffet box is priced at the dine-in salad bar price of THB 259 all day. Drinks and add-ons keep their menu prices, and the side is free exactly as it is with a steak at the table.

    Main course

    Menu price, from 199 for a kid's plate to 999 for the Fire Grilled Jaew Striploin.

    Buffet box, THB 259

    Harvest salad, soup, fresh bake and sweet bar, packed from the same bar the dine-in guest walks up to. Priced at what that bar costs all day in the restaurant.

    Drinks and add-ons

    Menu price. A fried egg is 19 at the table and 19 on the app. One side dish is free either way.

    A worked example

    Thai Jaew Fire Grilled Chicken at 299, the buffet box at 259, a Deep Green cold-pressed at 129, free side, no add-on. THB 687 for a full table at home.

    Delivery platforms take a commission of roughly a third, so holding price parity is a decision to make on purpose rather than by default. The alternative is a small platform-only uplift, at the cost of the promise above.

    How it reaches people

    The brand lives in four places at once, and each one does a different job.

    Own ordering page

    Where the set builder lives and where the margin is best. Every other channel points back here.

    Delivery platforms

    Listed as complete sets, not loose dishes, so the format is understood even by a first-time buyer scrolling past.

    Social and creators

    The unboxing of a composed set is the content. Guests show the set they built, which advertises the choice mechanic for free.

    The restaurant itself

    Dine-in guests are taught the set language at the table so the virtual version feels familiar when they order from home.

    Rollout

    Four stages, each one testing a single assumption before spending on the next.

    1. Month 1 to 2

      Build the set and prove it travels

      Lock the eight categories, test packaging and holding times, and confirm the food arrives the way it left the kitchen.

    2. Month 3

      Soft launch to existing guests

      Offer the Virtual Buffet to the current customer base first. Cheapest audience to reach and the most honest feedback.

    3. Month 4 to 6

      Open the channels

      Publish on delivery platforms, start creator partnerships, and push the set builder as the thing worth trying.

    4. Month 7 onward

      Extend the set, not the menu

      Seasonal categories and festive sets reuse the same kitchen. Growth comes from new combinations rather than new operations.

    How success gets measured

    Four numbers decide whether the format works, tracked from the first week.

    Average order value per set

    Does composing a set lift the basket above a normal delivery order?

    Share of Signature and Full Table sets

    Do guests trade up once they understand what the categories do?

    Repeat rate within 30 days

    Does the variety keep people coming back without discounts?

    Own channel share of orders

    How much volume moves off the platforms and onto our own page?
